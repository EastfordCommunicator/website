All members are participating in a mandated physical examination in

Eastford’s upcoming social event for all ages alike is Halloween!
Ghosts, ghouls, and monsters aren’t the only things to be afraid of on
Halloween. Accidents and mishaps increase dramatically when children
trick-or-treat. To avoid the many dangers children and adults face while
trick-or-treating, use common sense. Be aware of potential hazards and
take precautions to eliminate them. Consider participating in sanctioned

In keeping with NFPA Standards, smoke alarms at home, carbon monoxide
safety, and escape planning should be the norm for the fall and winter
seasons.

- Smoke Alarms at Home: Smoke alarms are a key part of a home fire
escape plan. When a fire starts, smoke spreads fast. Working smoke
alarms give you early warning so you can get outside quickly. Fact:
Smoke alarms should be installed inside every bedroom, outside each
sleeping area, and on every level. Roughly two out of three fire deaths
happen in homes with no working alarms or not working. Test alarms at
least once a month, replace conventional batteries when the time
changes, and replace any smoke alarm that is more than 10 years old.

- Escape Planning: Plan ahead! If a fire breaks out in your home, you
may have only a few minutes to get out safely once the smoke alarm
sounds. Everyone needs to know what to do and where to go if there is a
fire. Fact: Only one of every three households has actually developed
and practiced a home fire escape plan. Practice the escape plan at least
twice a year when seasons change.

- Carbon Monoxide Safety: Often called the silent killer, carbon
monoxide is an invisible, odorless, colorless gas created when fuels
such as gasoline, wood, coal, natural gas, propane, oil, and methane
burn incompletely. In the home, heating and cooking equipment that burns
fuel can produce carbon monoxide. Fact: A person can be poisoned by a
small amount of CO over a longer period of time or by a large amount of
CO over a shorter amount of time.
