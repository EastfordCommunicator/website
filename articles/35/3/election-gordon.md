---
title: Election Questionnaire for Jeff Gordon, CT State Senate District 35
---

**In your opinion, what are some of the most important issues facing the
35th Senate District, and Eastford specifically?**

“As a state senator, I have focused on affordability, with more than 35
actions to provide relief to people, small businesses, and our towns.
Examples include the income tax cut, expanded tax exemptions for
retirement accounts, child and caregiver tax credits, day care funding,
lower taxes on electric bills, and reining in prescription drug prices.
I stopped millions of dollars in education cuts. I helped increase
education funding, town aid for roads, and money from the casino fund to
ease pressure to raise property taxes. I have more than 25 affordability
proposals to help people. We must repurpose taxpayer money to fund core
government responsibilities, while still cutting taxes so people keep
more of their money upfront.”

**How do you hope to tackle these issues if elected to the State
Senate?**

“I continue my strong work ethic for which I am known as a state
senator, and which guided me during my 16 years as a town official in
Woodstock. Through my family, professional, and community experiences, I
understand what is going on, which helps me work with others on the
details of what can be done. I show up to get work done and to get
things done. I have a track record of working with people to find
common-sense solutions for our shared issues and concerns, while
supporting our values, individual and parental rights, and medical
freedoms to decide, as law-abiding adults, what is best for ourselves
and our families.”

**Why did you decide to run for election to the State Senate?**

“I was strongly recruited by the state senators before me and by many
people in the community because of my 16 years of leadership on
Woodstock’s Planning & Zoning Commission and as a state physician
leader. I wanted to further my work for our communities. During the past
four years as state senator, I have been involved in many different
issues and have helped people directly through that work and through
constituent services. This dovetails with my years of work helping
people as a doctor.”

**Talk about your background. What makes you stand out as the best
choice for State Senator?**

“I believe that my proven track record of common sense, working with
others, listening to people, and working for people to get things done
stands out clearly as to why I am the best choice to continue as state
senator. I have life experiences that my opponent does not have at his
young age. I have shown up to do the work and get things done for
everyone I represent. I spend a lot of time in all 13 towns of the
35<sup>th</sup> Senate District, which is not just a way for me to
connect with people, but also to bring to my work at the Capitol an
informed, grassroots perspective.”

**Can you share a personal story or experience that highlights your
commitment to public service?**

“My many years as a doctor show me every day why it is important to
fight to put people over profits, fight against insurance companies, and
fight against corporatization of healthcare and hospitals. One of the
many experiences I am proud of was when I fought and won against an
insurance company that denied, time and again, lifesaving surgery for a
constituent of mine. He now has an opportunity to live and to be with
his family.”

**What is the most important thing you would say to an undecided voter
heading to the polls on Nov. 3?**

“These are serious times. It requires serious people. It requires
fighting for what is right and against what is wrong. It requires common
sense. I have shown myself time and again to be that person during my
years as a doctor, during my prior years as a town public official, and
currently as a state senator. Leadership with experience matters. You
cannot replace it, especially with people who do not have it.”

**1. Coffee or tea?** Coffee

**2. Early bird or night owl?** Night owl

I like so many of them, so it is hard to choose — but I love pizza

by A-Ha, and “Born to Run,” by Bruce Springsteen

**5. What was your favorite subject in school?** Science

**6. What’s one TV show you could watch over and over?** MASH

Math and reading books.

1970’s.
