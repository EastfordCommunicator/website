---
title: Election Questionnaire for Pat Boyd, House District 50
---

**In your opinion, what are some of the most important issues facing the
50<sup>th</sup> District, and Eastford specifically?**

“Education funding and municipal support top that list. The funding
debate in Hartford often centers on urban centers versus wealthy suburbs
or Fairfield County. Rural Connecticut and small towns like Eastford,
with limited tax bases, often fall through the cracks. Much of my time
is spent educating my colleagues about rural Connecticut, including that
Windham County is the poorest county in the state, that our schools and
municipal budgets are lean and efficient, and that towns like Eastford
should not be lumped in with other communities. Additionally, the cost
of energy remains something the next Legislature must address,
specifically by expanding our energy sources, as rates will never go
down if we can’t source energy at a much lower rate, likely requiring a
multi-state coalition. In my role as House Chairman of the Public Safety
& Security Committee, I continue to work to give towns options to
support volunteer fire departments, quality EMS response, and to grow
the ranks of the Connecticut State Police, who are strained in the Troop
D region.”

**How do you hope to tackle these issues if elected to the House of
Representatives?**

“Tackling these issues requires building upon the foundation we have
established in past legislative sessions while maintaining a firm
commitment to fiscal responsibility. Over the last nine years, I have
held the line on taxes and continued to pay down our state's legacy debt
(we have paid down $11 billion since 2018). At the same time, we must
direct investment where it is needed most. I will work to expand the
Special Education Fund established last year, ensure the Education Cost
Sharing (ECS) grant is fully funded, and advocate for an updated ECS
formula that incorporates a dedicated “Rural Multiplier.” This
structural adjustment will ensure that small, rural communities like
Eastford receive their fair share of state resources to offset
geographic, high school tuition, and transportation challenges without
shifting costs onto local property taxpayers. Part of my job is ensuring
our communities can access essential state programs, including Small
Town Economic Assistance Program (STEAP) grants, LoCIP, and Town Aid
Road funds. I take a hands-on approach to local priorities, such as
spearheading the effort to secure state funding for the Eastford
Independent Fire Company to repair extensive exterior structural damage,
fix rotted studs, and install energy-efficient windows. Moving forward,
I will continue securing targeted investments for our volunteer first
responders and local infrastructure.”

**Why did you decide to run for election to the House of
Representatives?**

“In 2016, the incumbent state representative for the 50th District
announced his retirement, and I have always felt that serving your
community is important. At the time, I served as President of the
Pomfret Volunteer Fire Company and Deputy Emergency Manager for the
town. I had never sought elected office before, but I recognized and
appreciated that public service matters, and the Connecticut Legislature
requires citizen-legislators to serve on behalf of their communities. I
never wanted to be a ‘full-time politician,’ so I stood up and felt that
I could serve my neighbors by being a voice for my home and rural
Connecticut. It has been a rewarding but extremely challenging role, as
the political climate makes collaboration difficult and
conscience-driven decisions are required. In short, it has been the
honor of my life to serve in the Connecticut House of Representatives,
and not a day goes by that I take the responsibility that you have given
me for granted.”

**Talk about your background. What makes you stand out as the best
choice for State Representative?**

“I have a unique background. I grew up in the Quiet Corner and have
lived and worked here my entire life. After graduating from Plainfield
High School, I attended Eastern Connecticut State University, earning a
B.A., then graduated from Sacred Heart University, where I earned an
M.A. I am a career educator and school administrator, having worked in
Sterling before moving to the private sector, first at The Rectory
School and, for the last 20 years, at Pomfret School, where I am
currently the Associate Dean of Students, history teacher, and coach.
For the last 21 seasons, I have served as Camp Director at the June
Norcross Webster Scout Reservation in Ashford, the largest Scout Camp in
Connecticut, with more than 2,000 campers a season, 95 staff members,
and a large budget. I continue to volunteer as a firefighter in Pomfret
and am involved in the town’s Emergency Management efforts. In my nine
years in the Legislature, I have held leadership positions on the
Committee on Children, Veterans and Military Affairs, and for the last
four years I have served as the House Chairman of the Public Safety &
Security Committee. This background managing a large non-profit
business, education administration, and emergency management puts me in
a unique position to understand the work needed to address the unique
challenges facing Eastford and our region.”

**Can you share a personal story or experience that highlights your
commitment to public service?**

“After joining the Volunteer Fire Service in 2008, I realized how
fragile the system is, from dispatch to mutual aid volunteer response,
ambulance availability, and the growing need for training. We have
dedicated volunteers, but they need support to sustain the service. I
have dedicated a significant portion of my service in the State House to
developing solutions to recruit and retain firefighters, EMS responders,
and law enforcement officers. I’ve been on calls in the middle of the
night when an immediate response is delayed, and I’ve seen amazing,
hero-like work happen every day without the public noticing. My
commitment to these issues is directly shaped by my own experience on
the ground.”

**What is the most important thing you would say to an undecided voter
heading to the polls on Nov. 3?**

“The year 2026 is a highly politically polarized environment. We all
watch Washington with curiosity. Connecticut is different. It’s all
about the effort that you put into the work. I pride myself on having
built a reputation as a legislator who builds coalitions, does not toss
partisan mud, and, as a result, I chair one of the most bipartisan
committees in the State House. This isn’t because of the committee
content — it’s because I treat my counterparts with respect and give the
opposition a seat at the table in all decision-making. Being in the
majority means you have a responsibility to govern, and I have a seat at
the table as the House, Senate, and the governor make major decisions.
Although I don’t get everything I want despite my advocacy, I have been
able to influence major policies like our fiscal guardrails, agriculture
focus, workforce development, local funding, and healthcare
availability, and I am directly involved in the UConn Health/Day Kimball
partnership. Without someone like me in the room, decisions will be made
by those who represent cities, suburbs, and the Gold Coast, which I fear
will adversely affect rural communities like Eastford.”
