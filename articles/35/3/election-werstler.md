title: Election Questionnaire for Ethan Werstler, CT State Senate District 35
---
**In your opinion, what are some of the most important issues facing the
35<sup>th</sup> Senate District, and Eastford specifically?**

“In conversations with residents, I hear many issues of great
importance. Among them are rising property taxes levied to cover the
growing cost of education and special education. Connecticut has relied
too heavily, and for too long, on property taxes to cover most of the
costs of educating the next generation. This reliance pits our seniors
on fixed incomes against our parents. It pits our working families
against our students. We need to dramatically reform our education cost
sharing (ECS) formula to better serve small towns like Eastford and
others in my district. Small towns are getting crushed under the current
system. The path forward is to tie the ECS formula to inflation and
dramatically update its per-pupil amount. Big city politicians far away
aren’t listening to our elected officials, and I am concerned the
governor’s Blue-Ribbon Commission won’t properly consider the interests
of small towns.”

**How do you hope to tackle these issues if elected to the State
Senate?**

“For the past 30 years, there have been only fleeting moments where
Eastford and the 35th Senate District have been represented by the
majority party in the State Senate. Being relegated to the (“Super”)
Minority party has kept Eastford and this district from the table of
decision-makers in Hartford. I would join Democrats like State Rep. Pat
Boyd and other rural legislators, and work within the majority party to
deliver on important issues. Despite being nominated by the majority
party, I would be dedicated to working on a bipartisan basis to deliver
for my community.”

**Why did you decide to run for election to the State Senate?**

“Growing up in this district, a proud son of Woodstock, I got to live
the American Dream. To live down the road from my grandparents, in a
community we could afford, was a blessing. But that same opportunity has
become increasingly out of reach for seniors and young families in our
district. Knocking on thousands of doors across our community, I hear a
shared concern: ‘Does my family’s Eastern Connecticut story end with
me?’ We need something more. And soon. As a young person pursuing public
service, I see so many of my peers turn away, believing this work is not
meant for them. I believe anyone possessing the means and will to serve,
regardless of age, must answer that call. I accepted this great
challenge with open eyes and a full heart. The opportunity to serve my
community would be the greatest honor of my life.”

**Talk about your background. What makes you stand out as the best
choice for State Senator?**

“As an alum of Woodstock Academy and UConn, I am a proud product of
Eastern Connecticut’s public schools. During my time at UConn, I served
on the university’s Board of Trustees, representing tens of thousands of
undergraduate students, and upheld my fiduciary responsibility to the
state’s flagship university. I also fought for food-insecure students by
creating a food assistance program called “Husky Market.” After
graduating, I spent four legislative sessions working in government
relations in Hartford. Last year, I started a small business, coaching
high school and college students about public speaking, negotiations,
and leadership. I have mentored thousands of high schoolers and plan to
continue doing so when elected. What do I hope stands out? I don’t have
tons of money. I don’t have a big mansion. I am just a young man,
navigating young adulthood, hoping to dedicate myself to your best
interests.”

**Can you share a personal story or experience that highlights your
commitment to public service?**

“My grandparents were in their early 20’s when they moved to Woodstock.
By that time, they had two young kids and had bought a house they could
afford in Woodstock Valley. Decades later, those two kids — my dad and
my aunt — moved out and right down the street into Woodstock homes of
their own. The greatest joy of my life was growing up with my family
nearby. But when my grandparents talk about the future, they are looking
for a place better suited for senior living. There are practically no
accessible senior housing options in this part of the state. My cousins,
and so many of my friends, have moved away because they feel that there
is no place for them in Eastern Connecticut. My commitment to public
service extends not just to my family, but to every person who has made
the difficult but worthwhile choice to stay here.”

**What is the most important thing you would say to an undecided voter
heading to the polls on Nov. 3?**

“I would ask them the same thing I ask any person I meet on the campaign
trail: “What do you expect from your state government?” Now, more than
ever, we need a senator who can work within the supermajority. We need
someone who can influence the decisions being made and one who will not
be perpetually relegated to the minority. For too long, this district
has been denied a seat at the table in Hartford. I ask undecided voters
to expect more, to look elsewhere in our state, and to see how other
areas are better served with different representation. Real tax dollars
and state investment flow from your pockets to Hartford, and then to
other places in this state. I’d ask the undecided, perhaps unaffiliated
voter, to finally expect more, for a change.”
