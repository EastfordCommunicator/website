---
title: Tips for Today
author: Liz Ellsworth
---

Happy autumn! There are a lot of mushrooms in the woods right now, with
all kinds of striking colors (purple, orange, red, gold) and artistic
shapes (ones that look like coral reefs or a fan). They have creative
names like Chicken-of-the-Woods and Beefsteak. You’ll see them on the
ground, on a fallen, decomposing log, and in a tree’s upper branches,
especially after a good amount of rain.

There are some easy-to-use guides that fit in your pocket and have
excellent illustrations. These guides are especially helpful if you’re
opting out of using devices or, if we’re being real, there is no cell
service out on the trails. There is a colorful, waterproof fold-out from
earthskywater.net called “Common Mushrooms of the Northeast,” or, “The
Mushroom Finder,” by Jacob Kalichman and illustrated by Roo Vandegrift.
There are likely some groups of mycologists in your community. Always
consult an expert if you are planning to forage. As autumn progresses,
mushroom variety and abundance begin to dwindle due to temperature
changes and early-season snow cover.

Happy exploring!
