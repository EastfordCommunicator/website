The EIFC wishes all a safe and wonderful summer season. We responded to
15 "911" calls during the month of June.

It is with sadness that we announce the passing of two of our members
--- William Hull, who joined in 2014; and Ernie Nowakowski, who joined
in 1981. Ernie had a long and active membership as a fireman. In
addition, he served as Fire Lieutenant from 1985-1987, Fire Captain from
1987-1991, Treasurer from 1978 to 1980, and was on the Board of
Directors from 1991-1995.

Congratulations and thanks are in order! Congratulations to Eastford
Elementary School graduate Ryan J. Collard, who received the EIFC's 66th
Citizenship Award. This award is presented to a student who demonstrates
leadership, respect for school and community rules, and involvement in
school and community affairs.

Congratulations to Gavin Driscoll, who has successfully met his
probationary requirements and attained active membership status.

Congratulations to Dan Driscoll, who has successfully met the
requirements to become Fighter I. During the previous six months, he has
attended classes every Tuesday and Thursday, as well as every other
Saturday. FFI training is designed to equip firefighters with the
essential knowledge and skills to advance their careers in the fire
services, as mandated by NFPA 1001 standards, and includes both online
and hands-on components.

Thank you to the Quiet Corner Garden Club of Woodstock for providing
plants for the flower bed in front of the firehouse. A picture of the
flower bed accompanies this article.

Drills included fire engine pumping skills and newborn emergency care.
Fire engine pumping is essential for pump operators to effectively
manage water supply and ensure successful firefighting operations. Pump
operators must establish a reliable water supply, manage hose lines, and
perform various tasks efficiently. Understanding the apparatus and its
capabilities is fundamental to their role. Training also includes
scenarios that present challenges such as equipment failures or
unexpected changes in water supply. This drilling prepares the operators
to think critically and adapt to real-world situations.

Newborn emergency care for the first 28 days, or four weeks, after
delivery requires a combination of medical knowledge, practical infant
care skills, and interpersonal abilities to provide safe and supportive
care for newborns. Skills include basic neonatal care, including
recognizing signs of distress or illness, CPR, and infant first aid. EMS
is also trained in obstructive emergencies and transport protocols.
