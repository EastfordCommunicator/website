---
title: "Echoes of 1776: Residents Unite for America250 Reading of Declaration of Independence"
author: Deb Richards
---
*Eastford joins statewide celebration of the Declaration of
Independence*

EASTFORD --- On the evening of Wednesday, July 8, Eastford joined
communities across Connecticut in a special statewide commemoration
marking the day the Declaration of Independence was first publicly read
throughout the original 13 colonies in 1776. At exactly 6 p.m., towns
across the state gathered in unison to honor this historic moment as
part of the nation's continuing celebration of America's 250th
Anniversary.

Eastford's observance, held on the steps of the Ivy Glenn Memorial, was
intimate in size but rich in meaning. Residents gathered to reflect on
the ideals of liberty, equality, and self-government that have shaped
the nation for two and a half centuries.

The evening began with welcoming remarks from Deb Richards, who shared
the history behind the July 8 observance. While July 4 marks the
adoption of the Declaration by the Continental Congress, it was on July
8, 1776, that the document was first read aloud to the public, allowing
citizens throughout the colonies to hear the words that declared
America's independence.

One of the evening's most memorable moments came as Benjamin Torcellini
delivered a stirring reading of the entire Declaration of Independence.
His expressive delivery and commanding presence gave renewed life to the
timeless words penned nearly 250 years ago. As he proclaimed, "We hold
these truths to be self-evident, that all men are created equal," the
audience was reminded of the enduring ideals upon which the nation was
founded.

Following the reading, Bev and Bruce Lindemann led those gathered in a
heartfelt rendition of "America the Beautiful," inviting the community
to join together in song. The program continued with Phil Finch,
President of the Eastford Historical Society, who presented "Eastford: A
Historic Perspective," highlighting the town's rich history. Adding to
the significance of the occasion, Mark Sheldon Sr., representing the
Sons of the American Revolution, presented the Town of Eastford with a
beautifully framed copy of the Declaration of Independence. The document
is now proudly displayed in the lobby of the Town Office Building, where
it will serve as a lasting reminder of the freedoms and principles that
continue to unite the community.

Although modest in scale, Eastford's July 8 celebration reflected the
pride and patriotism of a town committed to remembering its past while
looking toward the future. The evening served not only as a tribute to
the nation's founding but also as a reminder that the ideals expressed
in the Declaration of Independence remain as relevant today as they were
in 1776.

The success of the event was made possible through the dedication of
many community members. Special thanks are extended to Benjamin
Torcellini, Bev and Bruce Lindemann, Phil Finch, and Mark Sheldon Sr.
for sharing their time, talents, and enthusiasm to create such a
meaningful program. Appreciation is also extended to everyone who
attended and helped commemorate this historic milestone as Eastford
joined communities across Connecticut in honoring the birth of our
nation.
