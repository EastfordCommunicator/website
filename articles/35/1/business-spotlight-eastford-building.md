EASTFORD --- "I never thought that I would own a lumber yard in my
lifetime," Ray Bergeron said. But then he did --- and has been the owner
of Eastford Building Supply for the past 21 years.

Eastford Building Supply (EBS) was founded by Ed Trepal in 1955. Trepal
was a contractor who decided to start selling products, and he built a
small building at the present location. That original building --- which
is now at the center of the store --- has since been added onto eight
times. In 1960, EBS was bought by Jack and Ned Hopkins, two brothers
originally from Providence, R.I. The boys' dad owned a lumber yard in
Providence, which drew them into the business. One ended up moving to
Eastford, and the other to Woodstock. They built a big base of custom
builders, and focused on "quality product above and beyond their
competition," Bergeron said.

Bergeron is also from Rhode Island, and still lives there. Early on, he
managed two Grossman's building products stores there, then worked for a
few wholesale lumber distributors, which led him to call on EBS. In
2003, Jack Hopkins apparently recognized Bergeron's potential and lured
him away from the distributor to become store manager at EBS.

Fast forward to 2005, when Bergeron bought the business. He has kept the
focus on quality products, which he believes leads to a loyal customer
base. EBS now has 400 to 500 active customers and delivers all over the
area, including as far away as Cape Cod. Ninety percent of sales are to
contractors. Eventually, Ray's son Eric joined the business and now runs
the hardware department.

"What makes us so good --- besides quality products --- are the people,"
said Ray. "Knowledgeable people who know how to handle customers."

Like most lumber yards, he said, they don't have outside salespeople.
But they will go to a building site and take measurements for quoting
things like siding and roofing. EBS also has outside resources for truss
design and floor systems. In sum, EBS aims to have all necessary
materials and key services on hand for building homes.

## Expanding into New Areas

In 2011, EBS opened its kitchen design center. Two years later, Chuck
Bosquet joined the company as its kitchen designer. A second designer
has since joined him. About half of the kitchens are sold to retail
customers.

Bergeron is well aware of the surging prices of building materials over
the past year --- especially for cedar products --- an increase he
believes is partly due to the 15-percent tariff on the import of
Canadian wood products. A lot of what he sells is imported, so high
tariffs on aluminum and other products increase his costs. Higher-priced
oil affects products like paints, stains, and PVC trim. Most of the
price increases, however, have been market-driven.

"Companies get what they can get. They know what they can put on it. A
lot of it is a greed thing," he said.

It is a mystery to him that with so much competition in products like
composite decking, prices continue to rise. PVC board prices keep going
up because, although there seem to be many "manufacturers," there are
really only two companies who actually make the product. And
consolidation of lumber yards and suppliers to lumber yards ---
primarily by private equity firms --- is reducing both competition and
salespeople's product knowledge, he says.

Despite the ups and downs in the economy, when asked what keeps him up
at night, Ray answered: "Actually, nothing." That's what happens when
you do things right.

EBS continues to thrive, with 19 full-time employees --- six of whom
live in Eastford. He uses them year-round. During slow days or the
off-season, employees perform maintenance and other work on-site.

"It's the people who make the business," he said.
