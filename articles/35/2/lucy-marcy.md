---
title: After Five Decades, An Amazing Homecoming for Lucy Marcy
---

EASTFORD --- After nearly 50 years away, Lucy Marcy's headstone has
finally returned home to Eastford.

The remarkable journey began in mid-July when First Selectman Deb
Richards received a call from Elaine Hess, chairperson of the Montville
Cemetery Group. While organizing the basement of Montville Town Hall,
the group discovered Lucy Marcy's headstone, which they believe had
been stored there since the 1960s. Further investigation revealed that
the stone had been found years earlier along a back road in Montville
and brought to Town Hall for safekeeping. How it came to be abandoned
there may never be known.

With assistance from Montville Town Historian Jon Chase, the group began
researching the stone's origins. Using records compiled by Mike Carroll
of Rediscovering History, Inc., along with the Hale Index System, they
determined that Lucy Marcy had originally been buried in Deane Cemetery
in Eastford. Elaine Hess then contacted Deb Richards to arrange for the
headstone's return.

On the morning of July 20, Lucy Marcy's headstone was carefully reset
in Deane Cemetery, placed behind her footstone and near the grave of her
husband's first wife, Polly. Oren Marcy married Polly in 1797 and,
following her death, married Lucy in 1820. Polly's parents are also
buried in Deane Cemetery, along with Oren and Polly's 3-year-old
daughter, Esther.

Although the exact date of Polly Marcy's death remains unknown, Lucy
Marcy died in 1830. Despite extensive research, the burial location of
Oren Marcy, who died in 1828, has not been confirmed. It is believed
that he was buried in Deane Cemetery, but his headstone has never been
located.

The successful return of Lucy Marcy's headstone was made possible
through the dedication and collaboration of many individuals. Special
thanks go to the Montville Cemetery Group, Mayor Bunnell Sr., Montville
Town Historian Jon Chase, Mike Carroll of Rediscovering History, Inc.,
Eastford First Selectman Deb Richards, Chairman of the Inland Wetlands
and Watercourses Tom DeJohn, and Historian Tom Latham, whose combined
efforts ensured that Lucy's marker was finally returned to its rightful
place.

After nearly half a century away --- and nearly two centuries after her
death --- Lucy Marcy's headstone once again rests in Eastford, reunited
with her family. Her story is a remarkable reminder of the importance of
preserving local history and honoring those who came before us. This
long-overdue homecoming stands as a testament to what can be
accomplished through historical research, community collaboration, and a
shared commitment to preserving our shared heritage.
